This page walks you through pinning your first window to Always-on-Top. The whole workflow takes about ten seconds, and once you've done it once, you'll use it every day.

## Pin your first window

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make sure PinPoint is running">
    Look for the PinPoint icon in your Windows system tray — the notification area at the right end of the taskbar. If you see it, PinPoint is running and your hotkey is active.

    If the icon isn't visible, check the hidden icons area by clicking the **^** arrow in the taskbar corner. If PinPoint isn't running at all, launch it from the Start menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click a window to give it focus">
    Click the window you want to keep on top — for example, a calculator, a reference document, or a video player. The window just needs to be in focus (its title bar will be highlighted).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Alt+P to pin the window">
    Press **Alt\+P** (your default hotkey). A colored border appears around the window. That border means the window is now pinned to Always-on-Top — it will stay visible above every other window until you unpin it.

    <Tip>
      Your hotkey might be different if you changed it during setup. Check Settings to see your current hotkey.
    </Tip>
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Alt+P again to unpin">
    Press **Alt\+P** a second time while the pinned window is in focus. The colored border disappears and the window returns to normal stacking order — other windows can now appear in front of it again.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What the border means

The colored border is a visual indicator that a window is pinned. It appears as soon as you pin a window and disappears as soon as you unpin it. You can change the border color and thickness — or turn it off entirely — in **Settings \> Border**.
